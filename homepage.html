Active Recall is one of the most effective, science-backed study techniques. Rather than passively rereading notes or textbooks, Active Recall pushes your brain to retrieve information without prompts. This mental effort strengthens memory and improves retention.

STRUCTURE

Ideal For: Memorizing concepts, formulas, definitions, processes

Tools: Flashcards (physical or digital), notebooks, quiz apps

Time Required: Flexible, best in short focused sessions

Complements: Spaced Repetition, Pomodoro

Steps to Master Active Recall

STEP 1

Study the material intentionally.

Read through a topic thoroughly, but avoid highlighting aimlessly. Focus on understanding rather than memorizing. Use diagrams or concept maps to help with comprehension.

STEP 2

Close your materials and recall what you remember

Without looking at your notes, try to write down or say everything you remember. This could be definitions, processes, key points, or even drawings. This forces your brain to retrieve data, which strengthens memory.

STEP 3

Check your accuracy

Compare your recall with the original source. Highlight what you missed or misunderstood. These gaps are your focus points for the next session.

STEP 4

Repeat recall regularly

Don't just do it once. Test yourself again after a few hours, the next day, or in combination with Spaced Repetition. Each attempt strengthens your neural pathways.

STEP 5

Use active tools

Use apps like Gizmo or Quizlet, or simply turn your notes into Q&A form. Make sure you're recalling actively—not just re-reading answers.

Congratulations, you have mastered Active Recall!

This Pomodoro Technique boosts focus by using 25-minute intervals of work, separated by short breaks. It prevents burnout and enhances productivity.

STRUCTURE

Ideal For: Focused study, writing, solving problems

Tools: Timer, apps like Forest or Focus Keeper

Time Required: 25 minutes + 5 min break

Complements: Active Recall

Steps to Master Pomodoro

STEP 1

Choose a specific task

Pick exactly what you're going to work on—be specific.

STEP 2

Set a 25-minute timer

Focus solely on your task until the timer rings.

STEP 3

Work with full focus

Ignore distractions. Jot down any that come to mind and return to them later.

STEP 4

Take a 5-minute break

Relax and recharge. Walk, stretch, or rest your eyes.

STEP 5

Take a longer break after 4 Pomodoros

Rest for 15–30 minutes to recover and retain energy.

Congratulations, you have mastered Pomodoro!

Spaced Repetition is a learning technique that involves reviewing information at increasing intervals to optimize long-term retention.

STRUCTURE

Ideal For: Long-term memorization, language learning, medical studies

Tools: Anki, SuperMemo, physical flashcards

Time Required: Daily short review sessions

Complements: Active Recall, Interleaved Practice

Steps to Master Spaced Repetition

STEP 1

Create your study materials.

Convert your study material into question-answer pairs or flashcards. Make them clear and concise.

STEP 2

Initial learning.

Learn the material thoroughly for the first time. Make sure you understand the concepts before moving to spaced review.

STEP 3

Review schedule.

Review the material at increasing intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days, etc. Adjust intervals based on your performance.

STEP 4

Track progress.

Keep track of which items you find difficult and which you've mastered. Focus more on the challenging items.

STEP 5

Update and grow your deck

As you cover new topics, keep adding them into your deck. Spaced Repetition becomes more powerful the longer you use it.

Congratulations, you have mastered Spaced Repetition!

The Feynman Technique is a method of learning that involves explaining concepts in simple terms to ensure deep understanding.

STRUCTURE

Ideal For: Understanding complex concepts, preparing for teaching

Tools: Whiteboard, paper, recording device

Time Required: 20-30 minutes per concept

Complements: Active Recall, Spaced Repetition

Steps to Master Feynman Technique

STEP 1

Choose a concept.

Select a concept you want to learn and write it down at the top of a blank page.

STEP 2

Explain it simply.

Write an explanation of the concept as if you were teaching it to someone who has no background in the subject.

STEP 3

Identify gaps.

Review your explanation and identify any areas where you struggled to explain clearly or had to use complex language.

STEP 4

Simplify and review.

Go back to the source material to fill in your knowledge gaps. Simplify your explanation further and review until you can explain it clearly.

STEP 5

Review and repeat

Practice teaching it out loud. If possible, explain it to a friend or classmate and invite questions. If they understand and can repeat it back, you've nailed it. If not, revise and repeat the process.

Congratulations, you have mastered the Feynman Technique!

Interleaved Practice involves mixing different topics or types of problems during study sessions to improve learning and retention.

STRUCTURE

Ideal For: Math, science, language learning, sports

Tools: Mixed problem sets, flashcards, practice tests

Time Required: Longer sessions than blocked practice

Complements: Spaced Repetition, Active Recall

Steps to Master Interleaved Practice

STEP 1

Identify related topics.

Group related topics or skills that you want to practice together.

STEP 2

Create mixed practice sets.

Create practice sets that mix different types of problems or topics randomly.

STEP 3

Identify the method before solving

Before answering, ask: "What kind of problem is this? What method should I use?" This trains your brain to recognize problem types and strengthens decision-making.

STEP 4

Review what you got wrong

After solving a set, go over your answers and pinpoint any confusion. Did you misapply a method because topics were too similar? That's good—it means the technique is working.

STEP 5

Reflect and adjust

After each session, write down what you learned. Interleaving is challenging at first, but over time, it greatly improves your ability to switch between topics smoothly.

Congratulations, you have mastered Interleaved Practice!
